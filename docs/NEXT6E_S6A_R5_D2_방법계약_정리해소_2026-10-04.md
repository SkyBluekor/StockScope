## 3. Primary theorem findings

### 3.1 Phandoidaen & Richter (2022), nonsmooth functional-dependence empirical process

Primary source:
- Nathawut Phandoidaen, Stefan Richter, *Empirical process theory for nonsmooth functions under functional dependence*, Electronic Journal of Statistics 16(1), 2022.
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.08512
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1214/22-EJS2023

Relevant results:
- Theorem 2.3: general functional CLT for nonsmooth function classes under functional dependence.
- Assumption 3.3: locally stationary approximation `X_i ≈ \tilde X_i(u)` in an Lp-type norm and Hölder continuity in rescaled time.
- Corollary 2.6: functional convergence of a localized EDF at fixed `v∈(0,1)`.

Corollary 2.6 is the closest direct theorem to R5, but it explicitly requires:
- distribution functions that are Lipschitz continuous;
- a Lipschitz kernel with compact symmetric support;
- `v∈(0,1)`;
- functional-dependence decay `δ^X_{2s}(k) ≤ c k^{-α}`, with `α>1/s`;
- `nh→∞`, `h→0`.

StockScope observed features are integer-basis-point lattice variables with atoms/ties. Their marginal CDFs are step functions, not Lipschitz CDFs. Therefore Corollary 2.6 cannot be directly bound to the R5 observed process.

### 3.2 Mayer, Zähle & Zhou (2020), local empirical process

Primary source:
- Ulrike Mayer, Henryk Zähle, Zhou Zhou, *Functional weak limit theorem for a local empirical process of non-stationary time series and its application*, Bernoulli 26(3), 2020, 1891–1911.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.3150/19-BEJ1174

The corresponding thesis formulation provides:
- Theorem 1.2.4: weak convergence of a local empirical process in a weighted sup-norm.
- A1/A2: smooth compact-support kernel and `nb_n→∞`, `b_n→0`.
- A3: each relevant distribution has a Lebesgue density.
- A6–A9: conditional density differentiability / integrability / bounded density conditions.

This route is even less directly compatible with StockScope's atomic lattice law. It cannot serve as a direct substitute for the blocked Corollary 2.6 route.

### 3.3 Birr et al. (2017), local strict stationarity

Primary source:
- Birr, Volgushev, Kley, Dette, Hallin, *Quantile Spectral Analysis for Locally Stationary Time Series*, JRSS B 79(5), 2017.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/rssb.12231

This paper is useful for the distribution-level definition of local strict stationarity and allows time-varying univariate/bivariate laws without requiring global stationarity. It supports the R5 process concept.

It does not supply the StockScope localized lattice EDF confidence band, latest-boundary band, median/MAD outer projection, or all-anchor common-N calibration.

### 3.4 Kreiss & Paparoditis (2015), bootstrap

Primary source:
- Kreiss & Paparoditis, *Bootstrapping Locally Stationary Processes*, JRSS B 77(1), 2015.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/rssb.12068

The bootstrap is designed around locally stationary process pseudo-series and statistics expressible as functionals of the preperiodogram, preserving required second/fourth-order structure.

It is not a published bootstrap theorem for StockScope's atomic localized EDF indexed jointly by horizon, anchor time, later time, and CDF threshold. It therefore cannot be used as an automatic L8 calibration approval.

## 5. Why the lattice gap is substantive

It is tempting to say that a distributional transform solves the atom problem automatically. R4 could use that transform in a stationary proof because the exact indicator identity and the inherited mixing argument were explicitly reviewed.

For R5, a transform would also have to preserve all of the following simultaneously:

1. the local-stationarity approximation rate;
2. the functional-dependence coefficient/rate used by the chosen empirical-process theorem;
3. a time-varying marginal transform `F_u`;
4. the localized estimator's centering and bias;
5. the latest-time one-sided construction;
6. the all-time simultaneous index family.

No reviewed source establishes that complete chain for the current StockScope object. A plausible proof idea is not enough to mark L2 APPROVED.

## 6. Why latest-state inference is not a cosmetic issue

StockScope's product use makes `u=1` central. The closest direct localized EDF corollary is explicitly interior-time.

A symmetric kernel at the right boundary loses mass outside the observed time domain unless it is renormalized or replaced by a one-sided/boundary-corrected construction. That alteration changes normalization, bias and covariance and therefore requires its own theorem binding.

Forbidden shortcut:

```text
take last k rows
→ call it local reference
```

No numeric window length is selected in D2.

## 7. Why all-anchor/common-N remains blocked

R5 ultimately needs one confidence event over:

```text
horizon h
× anchor s
× later v
× threshold x
```

because `N*=min A` is selected after evaluating all eligible anchors.

A functional CLT for `x` at one fixed local time `v` does not imply simultaneous validity over a growing collection of local times and candidate anchors.

The bandwidth shrinks, so estimates centered at sufficiently separated local times become increasingly local. A valid common-N claim therefore needs an explicit high-dimensional/uniform approximation or a conservative simultaneous bound whose multiplicity is part of the theorem. Neither has been established here.
